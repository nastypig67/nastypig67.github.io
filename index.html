Artefact of the month

Fact file · October 2026

The Antikythera Mechanism

The world's oldest known analogue computer

Civilisation
Ancient Greek
Dating
Made around 150–100 BC, so it is roughly 2,100 years old.
Discovered
Recovered in 1901 by sponge divers from a Roman-era shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera. It is now in the National Archaeological Museum in Athens.
Purpose
A hand-cranked machine of at least 30 bronze gears that tracked the Sun and Moon, showed the phases of the Moon, predicted eclipses and counted down to the Olympic Games.
Surprising fact
It was so far ahead of its time that no machine with gears this complex is known for more than 1,000 years afterwards, until the astronomical clocks of medieval Europe.

Ruthless Romans

  • 01

    The Romans built concrete that could heal itself. Nearly 2000 years ago, the Romans incorporated lime clasts into some of their concrete, enabling the material to react with water and seal cracks. This could help some Roman architecture to outlast modern structures!

  • 02

    One Roman banquet included a dish made from 5000 bird tongues. The Roman elites could hold astonishingly extravagant banquets, including many exotic foods such as ostrich brains and peas with grains of gold inside them.

  • 03

    Some Romans used urine to clean their clothes. Roman laundries, known as fullonicae, used urine in cleaning wool clothing. As urine breaks down, it produces ammonia, which can remove grease and stains. In fact, urine became so commercially useful that Emperor Vespasian introduced a tax associated with its collection.

  • 04

    The Romans could build structures extremely quickly and efficiently, which gave them an edge in war. Julius Caesar's soldiers built a bridge across the Rhine river in just 10 days. Additionally, Roman soldiers could build a fortress in a single night.

  • 05

    The dome of the Pantheon in Rome, finished nearly 1,900 years ago, is still the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world.

Glorious Greeks

  • 01

    Athens created one of the world's first democracies around 508 BC, letting citizens vote directly on laws.

  • 02

    The first recorded Olympic Games were held at Olympia in 776 BC in honour of Zeus, king of the gods.

  • 03

    The twelve Olympian gods were believed to live on Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in Greece.

  • 04

    Spartan boys left home at the age of seven to begin years of tough military training.

  • 05

    Alexander the Great built an empire stretching from Greece to India, and died in 323 BC aged just 32.

Enigmatic Egyptians

  • 01

    The Great Pyramid of Giza was built around 2560 BC for the pharaoh Khufu and was the tallest human-made structure on Earth for nearly 4,000 years.

  • 02

    Mummification took about 70 days, and the brain was pulled out through the nose with a long hook.

  • 03

    Hieroglyphs were a mystery until 1822, when Jean-François Champollion cracked them using the Rosetta Stone, which is now in the British Museum.

  • 04

    Tutankhamun became pharaoh at around nine years old. His almost untouched tomb was discovered by Howard Carter in 1922.

  • 05

    Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid.

Cunning Chinese

  • 01

    In 221 BC, Qin Shi Huang conquered the rival kingdoms and became the first emperor of a united China.

  • 02

    His tomb is guarded by the Terracotta Army: over 8,000 life-size clay soldiers, each with a different face.

  • 03

    The Art of War, written by the general Sun Tzu around 2,500 years ago, is still read by military leaders and business people today.

  • 04

    Paper, the compass, gunpowder and printing are known as the Four Great Inventions of ancient China.

  • 05

    Measured with all its branches, the Great Wall of China is over 21,000 km long.

Masterful Mesopotamians

  • 01

    Mesopotamia means “the land between the rivers”: the Tigris and Euphrates, in what is now mostly Iraq.

  • 02

    The Sumerians invented cuneiform, one of the world's first writing systems, around 3200 BC by pressing wedge shapes into wet clay.

  • 03

    They counted in 60s, which is why we still have 60 seconds in a minute and 360 degrees in a circle.

  • 04

    King Hammurabi of Babylon carved 282 laws onto a stone pillar around 1754 BC, including the famous “an eye for an eye”.

  • 05

    The Epic of Gilgamesh, about a king searching for eternal life, is one of the oldest great stories ever written down.

04 — on the pitch

Football corner

  1. 01

    Top ten greatest footballers of all time

    1. 10

      Alfredo Di Stéfano

      He's an icon and a legend for Real Madrid. Born in 1926, he helped Real win 5 consecutive European Cups (the old version of the Champions League) with his goalscoring ability and versatility as a striker and a defender.

    2. 09

      Garrincha

      People usually talk about Pelé when it comes to the greatest player during Brazil's 1950s and 1960s World Cup successes, but what many people forget is the major contribution of another Brazilian in these victories — Garrincha. He revolutionised the right wing, using his magical feet to both create and score goals and becoming the first player to win the Golden Boot, Golden Ball and World Cup trophy all in one tournament. The Pelé–Garrincha duo was so effective that Brazil never lost a single match with them both on the pitch.

    3. 08

      Johan Cruyff

      He was a true footballing pioneer, voted as the European Player of the Century. During his time at Ajax and Barcelona, he introduced a concept that would change the beautiful game forever — Total Football. This was where players would temporarily change positions on the pitch to effectively confuse defenders and bring wing-backs up the pitch. His masterful tactics led him to become an effective manager.

    4. 07

      Ronaldinho

      Known as “The Entertainer”, Ronaldinho lightened up the football world in the 2000s and 2010s. His skill and creativity arguably remains unmatched throughout footballing history, and his dazzling dribbling, no-look passes and bending free-kicks shocked the world during his relatively short career. Additionally, the Brazilian's constant smile and pure football flair made him one of the most watchable players ever.

    5. 06

      Zinedine Zidane

      Zidane's elegance and ball control make him undoubtedly the greatest French footballer of all time. Unlike most dribblers of his era, he didn't rely on blistering pace or power — instead, the Frenchman glided past defenders with a real sense of calm and control. However, his trophy cabinet says it all: 1998 World Cup, 2000 Euros, 2002 Champions League, 1998 Ballon d'Or and three-time FIFA Player of the Year awards.

    6. 05

      Cristiano Ronaldo

      CR7 is perhaps one of the most prolific strikers of his generation. Known as the Champions League King, Ronaldo is the all-time top goalscorer in the Champions League, and has won the prestigious tournament a record 5 times. Moreover, Ronnie holds the record for the only player to win domestic league titles and MVP awards in the Premier League, La Liga and Serie A, showing his versatility and longevity.

    7. 04

      Diego Maradona

      Maradona is considered by many fans and footballers to be the greatest of all time. Together with Pelé, he was voted FIFA Player of the Century, and his ability to carry a mediocre team to glory was demonstrated at the 1986 World Cup and Napoli's first ever Serie A title.

    8. 03

      Ronaldo Nazário

      At his prime, Ronaldo Luís Nazário de Lima was a terrifying striker with fancy footwork, explosive pace and an unstoppable shot. He won his first Ballon d'Or when he was just 17, becoming the youngest ever to do so, and won the award a second time in 2002, when he powered Brazil to World Cup glory.

    9. 02

      Pelé

      Edson Arantes do Nascimento is widely considered as the GOAT. He is the only player ever to win three World Cups, reach over 1200 career goals and score in a World Cup final at only 17 years of age. The King of Football's athleticism, dribbling and goalscoring ability was decades ahead of his time.

    10. 01

      Lionel Messi

      Messi is the most complete forward in the history of the beautiful game. He was a dazzling dribbler, able to weave his way past entire teams and world-class defenders. At the same time, his vision and passing accuracy helped him reach over 420 career assists, the most ever. What's more, his goalscoring stats are somehow crazier than the last two: in 2012, Messi found the net a ridiculous 91 times, a record that surely will never be broken.
